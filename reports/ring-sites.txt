Ring-cursor constant scan -- tools/ring_sites.py

Section 7 of tales-blockcodec-doc gives 4078/4079/4080 as the shortcut and
says to run it first, before sweeping any data.  That order is followed
here.  All three immediates are asked for at once and the immediate is
printed for every hit, because which one answers says which copy of the
source a build descends from.

This report adds a second pass for 4070 and 4071.  On this disc the ring
clear is an inline byte loop unrolled by eight with a seven-byte tail, so
the *loop bound* is 4070 or 4071 and only the *cursor* carries 4078/4079.
The published shortcut still finds the routine -- the cursor is inside it --
but at +135 and +139 words rather than at +10, so a reader who disassembles
forty instructions around the hit lands past the dictionary setup entirely.

THE RING-CLEAR UNROLL IS THE COMPILER'S, NOT THE SOURCE'S
==============================================================
Four PlayStation 2 builds clear the ring with an inline loop.  They split
by compiler stamp, not by source lineage -- and Legendia is the row that
settles it, because its decoder shares 18 bytes with this one (the noise
floor) and unrolls the loop identically anyway.
